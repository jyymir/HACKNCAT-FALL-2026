LENOVO HEATMATCH - ROADMAP AND SCALABILITY PLAN
================================================
Prototype status as of 2026-11-14. Concept prototype; not a Lenovo product or endorsement.
Everything after "Phase 0" is a plan, not something that exists today.


1. WHERE WE ARE TODAY (PHASE 0 - HACKATHON PROTOTYPE)
-----------------------------------------------------
Built and working:
  - Calculation engine (TypeScript, 24 automated tests): recoverable heat, heat-pump
    electricity, delivered heat, costs, payback, CO2, transparent pass/fail rules.
  - Month-by-month supply vs. demand using real 2024 weather (Open-Meteo / ERA5,
    Greensboro NC area), shared heat allocation with no double-counting.
  - Cooling-technology comparison (air, rear-door, warm-water liquid / Neptune-style;
    45 C inlet from Lenovo Press DS0187).
  - Scenario save/compare, map, printable report, CSV.
  - Gemini AI: plain-language what-ifs (engine computes the before/after) and Q&A
    limited to the tool's own numbers.

Still illustrative:
  - 8 fictional buildings, cost and price assumptions, heat-pump efficiency heuristic,
    heat-capture shares, emissions factors.

Baseline demo result (10 MW site, monthly model):
  6.3 MW usable heat -> 25,905 MWh/yr actually used (57%), ~$1.48M/yr net savings,
  ~$11.8M setup, ~7.9-yr payback, ~2,920 t CO2/yr avoided (illustrative factors).
  Liquid vs. air cooling: ~$1.72M vs ~$1.13M per year; winter backup heating
  1,382 vs 8,432 MWh.


2. PHASED ROADMAP
-----------------

PHASE 1 - REAL DATA FOR ONE PILOT SITE (0-3 months)
  Goal: replace demo data with real inputs for one real data center and its neighbors.
  - Building discovery: pull nearby buildings from OpenStreetMap / county GIS
    (type, footprint, location) instead of hand-entered demo buildings.
  - Demand estimates: building-type energy intensities (e.g. U.S. EIA CBECS) x floor
    area, then calibrate with real utility bills where owners share them.
  - Weather: multi-year climate normals instead of one year (2024).
  - Data-center inputs: real IT load profile and cooling-loop temperatures from the
    operator (or Lenovo Neptune system specs for liquid-cooled sites).
  - Validation: compare screening results against one independent engineering study.
  Success measure: screening ranks the same top candidates as the engineering study.

PHASE 2 - ENGINEERING-GRADE SCREENING (3-9 months)
  - Shared heat networks: one trunk pipe branching to several buildings, compared with
    separate pipes (minimum-spanning-tree / Steiner-tree layout along streets).
  - Hourly matching and thermal storage: size a hot-water tank to move summer surplus
    into evenings and shoulder seasons.
  - Validated heat-pump performance curves from manufacturer data (replace heuristic).
  - Full project finance: NPV/IRR, financing, incentives (e.g. available tax credits and
    utility programs), heat-sale contract pricing.
  - Grid-emissions factors by region and hour (e.g. U.S. EPA eGRID).
  - Backend + database so projects, scenarios and audit trails are shared by a team.
  Success measure: results within an agreed tolerance of detailed feasibility studies.

PHASE 3 - PORTFOLIO AND PLATFORM (9-24 months)
  - Site selection: screen many candidate data-center sites at once and rank them by
    heat-reuse potential before land is bought.
  - Live mode: connect to data-center telemetry (actual heat output and loop temps) to
    track delivered heat and savings after a project is built.
  - Multi-region: climate, energy prices and regulations per country (EU heat-reuse
    rules are already pushing operators to evaluate reuse).
  - APIs for partners: utilities, district-heating operators, city planners.


3. WHY IT SCALES
----------------
Technical:
  - The engine is already a pure, tested function: inputs -> results. Running it for
    1 site or 1,000 sites is the same code; it runs in milliseconds per scenario.
  - Data sources needed at scale are public or standard: map/GIS data, building-energy
    surveys, weather archives, grid-emissions datasets, operator telemetry.
  - The AI layer orchestrates and explains; it does not do the math. Adding more AI
    use does not make results less reliable.

Market:
  - Every new AI data center has to remove all of its heat. U.S. data-center electricity
    was ~176 TWh in 2023 and is projected at 325-580 TWh by 2028 (DOE/LBNL); global
    ~415 TWh in 2024 to ~945 TWh by 2030 (IEA). Nearly all of that ends up as heat.
  - Screening is cheap and fast compared with a one-off engineering study, so it can be
    run on every site, not just the few that get studied today.

Rule of thumb from the demo: ~2,590 MWh of heat reused per MW of IT load per year
in the baseline scenario. This only scales when there is enough nearby demand, which is
exactly what the tool checks site by site.


4. HOW LENOVO COULD BRING IT TO MARKET (STRATEGIC FIT, NOT A COMMITMENT)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Pre-sales: run HeatMatch when customers evaluate liquid-cooled infrastructure, to
    show the heat-reuse upside of warm-water cooling at their actual site.
  - Services: a feasibility-screening offer that leads into detailed engineering with
    partners (district-heating firms, utilities, engineering consultancies).
  - Sustainability reporting: help customers quantify heat reused and emissions avoided.
  - Product feedback: aggregate (anonymized) results show which loop temperatures and
    capture rates unlock the most reuse, informing future cooling designs.


5. KEY RISKS AND HOW WE'D HANDLE THEM
-------------------------------------
  - Demand data is hard to get -> start from public intensities, calibrate with bills,
    always label modeled vs. measured.
  - Seasonal mismatch (summer surplus) -> storage, low-temperature summer customers
    (greenhouses, pools, industrial), or absorption cooling.
  - Pipe routes and permits dominate cost -> route analysis in Phase 2; screening flags
    distance-driven projects early so effort goes to the best candidates.
  - Over-trust in AI -> engine does all calculations; AI output is labeled and must be
    confirmed by the user before any setting changes.
  - Heat-sale contracts and ownership -> finance module plus partner models in Phase 2.


6. METRICS WE WOULD TRACK
-------------------------
  - Sites screened; candidates found per site; share that pass to detailed study.
  - MWh of heat actually reused per year; t CO2 avoided (with stated factors).
  - Accuracy vs. detailed studies (screening error on savings and payback).
  - Time from "new site" to "first answer" (target: same day).
