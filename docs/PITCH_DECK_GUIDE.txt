LENOVO HEATMATCH - PITCH DECK GUIDE
===================================
Built around the NC A&T x Lenovo problem statement and rubric:
  4 questions: Real problem / Who experiences it / What you'd build / Why Lenovo
  + Human & business value, Innovation, Impact
  Rubric: Problem & User 15 | Innovation 20 | Technical Execution 25 |
          Impact & Lenovo Relevance 20 | Feasibility & Scalability 10 | Pitch & Demo 10
  Technical Integrity requirement: say clearly what you built, what works, what's conceptual.

All numbers below come from the app (baseline scenario, monthly demand model) or from a
cited source. Re-check them in the app before presenting if you change any settings.


SLIDE 1 - TITLE
  "Lenovo HeatMatch: turning AI data-center heat into local warmth"
  Subtitle: waste-heat reuse planner. Team names. Small line: "Concept prototype".

SLIDE 2 - THE PROBLEM   (Problem & User - 15)
  - U.S. data centers: ~176 TWh in 2023 (~4.4% of U.S. electricity), projected
    325-580 TWh by 2028. [DOE / LBNL]
  - Global: ~415 TWh in 2024 -> ~945 TWh by 2030. [IEA]
  - Nearly every watt a server uses becomes heat, and it is usually thrown away.
  - Meanwhile hospitals, pools, greenhouses and apartments nearby pay to make heat.
  Say: "The heat is already there. The question nobody can answer quickly is:
        who nearby could actually use it, and would it pay off?"

SLIDE 3 - WHO EXPERIENCES IT AND WHY IT MATTERS
  - Data-center planners / sustainability leads: need a fast, defensible first answer
    before paying for an engineering study.
  - Building owners: heating bills and emissions targets.
  - Cities and utilities: AI growth vs. grid load and community benefit.
  Key insight (the "aha"): BEING CLOSE ISN'T ENOUGH. Temperature, demand, distance,
  seasons, electricity cost and cooling technology decide whether reuse works.

SLIDE 4 - WHAT WE BUILT   (Technical Execution - 25)
  One screenshot of Overview + one of Matches. List:
  - Ranks nearby buildings with plain-language reasons (good / possible / not a fit).
  - Month-by-month supply vs. demand from real 2024 weather.
  - Shares one heat supply without double-counting.
  - Cooling comparison: air vs. rear-door vs. warm-water liquid (Neptune-style).
  - Scenarios: save and compare side by side.
  - Gemini AI: plain-English what-ifs with an engine-computed before/after.
  - 24 automated tests; works offline (except map tiles and AI).

SLIDE 5 - HOW IT WORKS (keep it to one diagram)
  Data center (IT load, cooling) -> usable heat -> monthly supply
  Buildings (demand, temperature, distance) + weather -> monthly demand
  -> heat pump + pipe + cost model -> verdict + reasons -> shared allocation -> report
  Say: "The AI explains and proposes. The engine calculates. AI never invents numbers."

SLIDE 6 - RESULTS FROM THE DEMO SCENARIO (10 MW site)
  - 6.3 MW usable heat; 25,905 MWh/yr actually used (57%) ~= 2,160 homes' worth*
  - ~$1.48M/yr net savings, ~$11.8M setup, ~7.9-year payback
  - ~2,920 t CO2/yr avoided (illustrative emissions factors)
  - 3 good matches, 2 possible, 3 not a fit, each with a reason
    (e.g. laundry is close and big but needs 120 C; office park is too far)
  - Seasons matter: January is 2,243 MWh short; 79% of summer heat goes unused.
  * homes comparison is a rough illustration (~12 MWh/home/yr).
  Say clearly: "These buildings are fictional demo data. The method is what's real."

SLIDE 7 - WHY LENOVO   (Impact & Lenovo Relevance - 20)
  - Heat reuse depends on capturing heat in WARM water. Lenovo Neptune warm-water
    cooling supports inlet water up to 45 C and is designed so heat can be reused in
    building heating. [Lenovo Press DS0187]
  - In our model, liquid vs. air cooling (same site, monthly demand):
      savings ~$1.72M vs ~$1.13M per year (+53%)
      winter backup heating 1,382 vs 8,432 MWh
  - Lenovo has the infrastructure, services and customer reach to put a screening tool
    in front of customers at the moment they choose cooling.
  Wording: "strategic fit", "could help". Never say Lenovo endorses or offers this.

SLIDE 8 - WHAT'S DIFFERENT   (Innovation - 20)
  Today: one-off engineering studies, one site at a time, often after the design is set.
  HeatMatch:
  - Screens many buildings in minutes, before design decisions are locked in.
  - Explains WHY a building fails, not just a score.
  - Models seasons and cooling technology, the two things rules of thumb ignore.
  - AI what-ifs that are checked by a deterministic engine and need user approval.

  Modeled/illustrative: the 8 buildings, costs, prices, heat-pump curve, capture shares,

SLIDE 10 - ROADMAP & SCALABILITY   (Feasibility & Scalability - 10)
  Phase 1 (0-3 mo): one real pilot site, real buildings (OSM/GIS), CBECS intensities,
    utility-bill calibration, multi-year weather.
  Phase 2 (3-9 mo): shared networks, hourly + storage, validated heat-pump curves,
    NPV/incentives, regional emissions, team backend.

  Why it scales: the engine is a tested pure function (ms per scenario); inputs are

LIKELY JUDGE QUESTIONS (with honest answers)
  Q: Are these real buildings/numbers?
  A: No. The buildings are fictional demo data; weather and the Neptune 45 C figure are
     real and cited. Phase 1 replaces demo buildings with real GIS + utility data.
  Q: What about summer when nobody needs heat?
  A: That's why we model monthly. Baseline: 79% of summer heat is unused. Fixes: storage,
     low-temperature summer customers (greenhouses, pools), or absorption cooling.
  Q: Why does the hospital/greenhouse show more savings on its page than on Share heat?
  A: The building page is the individual estimate (if it were the only customer).
     Share heat splits one limited supply across everyone, month by month.
  Q: How accurate is the heat-pump model?
  A: It's a simple, labeled heuristic. Phase 2 swaps in manufacturer curves.
  Q: What does the AI actually do?
  A: It translates plain English into setting changes and explains results. Every number
     comes from our tested engine, and changes need the user's approval.
  Q: Why Lenovo and not any server company?
  A: Warm-water liquid cooling is what makes heat reusable; Lenovo publishes 45 C inlet
     operation and heat reuse for Neptune, and has the infrastructure/services reach.
     We frame it as strategic fit, not an existing Lenovo offering.
  Q: Pipe costs look simplified.
  A: Yes: straight-line distance x 1.3 route factor x $/m. Real routing and permits are
     Phase 2; the screening flags distance-driven projects early.


WORDING RULES (protects your Technical Integrity score)
  DO say: "modeled", "illustrative", "screening estimate", "strategic fit",
          "needs site-specific engineering".
  DON'T say: "Lenovo's product", "Lenovo endorses", "measured savings",
             "machine learning" (the demand model is degree-day rules, not ML),
             "digital twin" (it's a scenario simulator, not live monitoring).
